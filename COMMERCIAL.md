## Community License Eligibility

The community license permits use at no charge by:

- individuals acting on their own behalf
- organizations that meet **all three** requirements: **10 or fewer developers in total**, **less than $1,000,000 (USD) in gross revenue**, and **less than $1,000,000 (USD) in cumulative funds raised**

Count all current employees and contractors who perform software development for the organization, whether or not they use Locali. Entities under common control are counted together; their respective revenue and funding are aggregated, excluding transfers within the group to avoid double counting. For example, an organization with eleven developers does not qualify even if only one of them uses Locali.

Gross revenue is measured over the most recently completed fiscal year. Before the first fiscal year ends, measure revenue recognized from formation through the assessment date. Revenue eligibility is reassessed at each fiscal year end, or continuously during the first fiscal year; developer count and cumulative funding are assessed as they change.

Cumulative funding includes equity investments, capital contributions, debt financing, and funding grants received to date. It excludes ordinary customer payments already accounted for as revenue and is not reduced when funds are spent or financing is repaid. Funding remains a separate limit after revenue begins. Exactly ten developers qualifies; exactly $1,000,000 under either financial measure does not. Section 2 of the [community license](LICENSE.md) defines these requirements.

When using Locali on behalf of an employer or client, that organization must qualify for the community license or hold a commercial license covering that use, subject to the limited contractor access and contribution exception below. An individual's eligibility does not override the organization's requirements.

Eligible users may use Locali for commercial or non-commercial work, including checking proprietary source code and localization files locally or in CI. They may keep modifications private. Files checked by Locali do not become subject to its license merely because it processes them.

Eligible organizations may give copies and access to employees and contractors solely for work on their behalf. The organization is responsible for compliance and for ending access when the work or its permission ends. Contractors count toward its developer limit, but their own organization's size does not disqualify that limited access. Access does not extend to independent use or work for other clients.

Redistribution outside those exceptions requires a paid commercial agreement expressly granting those rights, regardless of developer count, revenue, or funding.

## When a Commercial License Is Required

Organizations with **more than 10 developers**, **$1,000,000 (USD) or more in gross revenue for the applicable measurement period**, or **$1,000,000 (USD) or more in cumulative funds raised** require a commercial license for ordinary use of Locali, including as a standalone CLI or in CI, unless covered by the transition period below. The contribution exception remains available regardless of these measures.

If an eligible organization ceases to meet any eligibility requirement, the community license allows continued ordinary use for **30 days from that change**, including when a financial measure reaches exactly $1,000,000. The measurement rules above determine when revenue eligibility changes. After that period, the organization must obtain a commercial license or stop ordinary use unless it again meets all requirements. An organization already ineligible when first seeking to use Locali receives no transition period. Platform permissions and the contribution exception remain available.

A paid commercial agreement expressly granting the relevant rights is required for redistribution outside the stated exceptions and for hosted Locali services for third parties. These requirements also apply to otherwise-eligible individuals and organizations. A license for internal use alone does not grant either right.

The following plans are available for commercial licensing:

A **seat** covers one named developer currently working for a covered entity who uses Locali directly or actively contributes code or localization content to a project checked by an automated Locali workflow. In shared CI, each current developer actively working on the checked project on that entity's behalf needs a seat while doing that work. Historical authors, former employees, and former contractors do not need seats merely because their old code is scanned. Independent external contributors do not need Customer seats solely because CI checks their submissions; their own direct use requires independently applicable permission. Build agents, service accounts, and people who only read reports do not need seats.

The organization's total developer count, revenue, and cumulative funding determine community eligibility. Current developers meeting the commercial seat criteria determine paid seat requirements. Section 3 of the commercial agreement governs assignment and reassignment.
